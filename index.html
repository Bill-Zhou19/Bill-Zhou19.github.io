I am a Ph.D. candidate in the School of Systems Science and Industrial Engineering at the State University of New York at Binghamton, advised by Prof. Fuda Ning in the Advanced Materials & Additive Manufacturing Lab.

I develop and integrate extrusion-based additive manufacturing (EAM) processes, with a focus on turning new process concepts into robust, scalable manufacturing solutions. My work spans multiple application areas, including solvent-free lithium-ion battery electrode manufacturing, EAM of continuous CFRP composites for improved structural performance, and EAM of tungsten-based alloys to study process-microstructure-property relationships. I combine hands-on experimentation with modeling and DOE-driven optimization, and I’ve contributed to research outputs including patent disclosures and conference publications.

I’m always open to collaborations in advanced manufacturing and process development!

RESEARCH EXPERIENCE

Jan 2025 – Jan 2026

A Novel Dry Manufacturing Process for Lithium-Ion Battery Electrode Fabrication

Research Assistant · Binghamton University · Advanced Materials & Additive Manufacturing Lab

Proposed a Rolling-assisted, Extrusion-based Dry (RED) process to eliminate toxic/expensive NMP solvent use in conventional slurry-based Li-ion battery electrode manufacturing.

Research details +
  • Developed a PVDF/CB/active-material (AM) filament feedstock and a custom monolithic deposition head for solvent-free extrusion, followed by powder spreading and heated rolling to form dense, high-loading electrodes.
  • Investigated how the RED sequence (extrusion-spreading-rolling) enables uniform powder distribution, robust interfacial bonding, and mechanically stable electrode architectures at high mass loading.
  • Evaluated electrochemical performance using both half-cell and full-cell configurations, and further demonstrated feasibility in pouch-cell (soft-pack) prototypes to assess practical scalability and application readiness.
  • The research findings were accepted to present at the 54th North American Manufacturing Research Conference (NAMRC 54) and published in Journal of Manufacturing Processes. The results have also been filed as a U.S. patent application.

Radio-frequency Assisted Additive Manufacturing of Carbon Fiber Composites

Research Assistant · Binghamton University · Advanced Materials & Additive Manufacturing Lab

Investigated RF-assisted additive manufacturing to improve interlayer bonding and mechanical performance of continuous carbon fiber reinforced thermoplastic composites (CCFTC).

Research details +

  • Established a foundation for a novel, non-contact process route toward high-strength CCFTC component fabrication.
  • The results were accepted for poster presentation at the 2025 MRS Smart Energy Symposium.
